# Scheduling Criteria

## Meaning
Scheduling criteria means the **standards or parameters** on the basis of which we decide how good, efficient, and fair a CPU scheduler’s decision is. In simple words, it is used to **judge how well the scheduler is using the CPU**.

---

## Criteria

### 1) CPU Utilization
It shows **how much time the CPU remains busy** and **how much time it stays idle**.  
Higher CPU utilization means better use of system resources.

---

### 2) Throughput
It means **how many processes are completed in a unit time**.  
Higher throughput indicates better system performance.

---

### 3) Turnaround Time
It is the **total time taken by a process from submission to completion**.  
Turnaround Time = Completion Time − Arrival Time

---

### 4) Waiting Time
It is the **total time a process spends waiting in the ready queue** before getting CPU time.

---

### 5) Response Time
It is the **time taken from process request submission until it gets CPU for the first time**.  
This is very important for interactive systems.

---

### 6) Fairness
Fairness means **every process gets an equal and justified chance to use the CPU**, and no process is treated unfairly.

---

### 7) Starvation Avoidance
Starvation happens when a process **does not get CPU for a very long time**.  
A good scheduler must avoid starvation.

---

### 8) Predictability
Predictability means the **execution behavior of a process can be estimated**.  
For example, a process should know that it will take at most a certain amount of time to complete.

---

### 9) Overhead
Overhead means the **extra time, CPU cycles, memory, or effort** spent by the system only on management tasks like scheduling and context switching, instead of doing actual useful work.

---

### 10) Scalability
Scalability means when **system load increases**, the system performance should also **grow smoothly and proportionally**, without collapsing or degrading badly.
